# Candidates — local judging

Judge brief (criteria in order): (1) on-subject excuse, one line, ≤20 words;
(2) almost believable — a real, specific, boring detail, not a wild story;
(3) funny on its own logic/detail; (4) reads like a hurried text — plain
words, no setup, no explanation, no signature; (5) fresh angle — not a dead
grandmother, a flat tyre or a sudden fever.

Field note: the competition opened with 0 submissions, so there is no public
field to scan yet; all 8 candidates below are original. The banned trio
(grandmother / flat tyre / fever) and the other worn lines (boiler, food
poisoning, "car won't start") were avoided or, where kept as a control,
marked down on criterion 5.

## Pairwise verdicts (criterion order, first difference decides)

- **B vs C** — (2) C's hole: jury summons arrive weeks ahead, so "letter only
  came yesterday" is exactly the detail a twice-burned manager catches. B has
  no hole: the school called this morning, lice, stay home. **B wins.**
- **B vs F** — (2) F's fumigation is vivid but slightly wild; B's school call
  is more mundane and more believable. (3) B is funnier: the manager recoils
  and asks no follow-ups. **B wins.**
- **B vs A** — (5) the boiler is the classic excuse, worn. B is fresh. **B wins.**
- **B vs D** — (2) D invites "use your phone hotspot"; B leaves the manager
  no counter-move. **B wins.**
- **B vs G** — (5) "no driving after the dentist" is a known line; B is
  fresher and funnier. **B wins.**
- **B vs E** — (2) "annual" check should have been announced in advance, so
  the manager wonders why no notice. B has no such hole. **B wins.**
- **B vs H** — (2) "plumber says stay home" is odd — why would the plumber
  say that? B's logic is airtight. **B wins.**
- **C vs F** — (2) jury duty is official and unarguable on the day, but the
  letter timing is a hole; F is hole-free but wilder. C edges it on
  believability. **C is runner-up.**
- **F vs A** — (5) bed bugs beat the boiler on freshness; (3) F is funnier.
  **F third.**
